Workshop notes

Why tolerance is everything

A print-in-place mechanism is printed already assembled, so the gap between moving parts is decided at design time — too small and the parts fuse into a paperweight, too large and the action feels sloppy. Every printer, nozzle and filament lands differently, which is why this tool asks for your number rather than guessing.

The cycloidal drive

A lobed disc rolls around a ring of pins, driven by an eccentric cam under the knob. One full turn of the knob advances the disc by a single lobe — a quietly hypnotic reduction of 7:1. The disc is captured under the wall lip and the knob, so nothing escapes. The preview animates exactly this: the disc's drunken wobble is the mechanism working.

The planetary gears

A sun gear, a ring gear and a set of planets, all cut as herringbones. The chevron teeth lock the parts together vertically — no pins, no caps — which is what makes the set printable in one piece. Spin the sun knob and the planets orbit, each spinning on its own axis as it goes.

Printing

PLA or PETG, 0.2 mm layers, no supports, no brim needed. After printing, work each part loose gently — a few firm twists breaks the first-layer bond. If it will not free, print the test plate and move one step looser.

Field manual

Astounding Shape Designer

A field manual for the superformula slicer

1 · What this is

Astounding Shape Designer turns a mathematical surface into a set of flat parts you can cut on a laser and slot together into a three-dimensional object. It does the geometry, the joinery and the sheet layout, and gives you SVG cut files in millimetres.

The surface is a superformula — a single equation that produces starfish, seed pods, urchins, crystals, bowls and vases depending on seven numbers. The app sweeps it twice: once around the equator to give each layer its outline, and once from pole to pole to give the stack its profile.

Everything is measured in millimetres, and the SVG files are true to size. Do not scale them on the way to the laser.

2 · Quick start

  • Pick a preset in section 2, or hit Randomise and press Back if you regret it.
  • Set the diameter and height you want in section 1 — or switch to Fit to sheets and let it size itself to the material you have.
  • In section 5, set Material to the real thickness of your ply, measured with callipers, not the number on the label.
  • Cut a kerf test comb (see Joinery) and set the Kerf slider to what you measure.
  • Turn on Hollow rings if you want a vessel rather than a solid stack, and set the Wall.
  • Choose your ribs: how many, how they join, how deep they grip.
  • Read the summary line under the preview. It will tell you if something has been quietly dropped.
  • Switch to Cut sheets, check the nesting, then Download cut sheets (SVG).

3 · How it goes together

The object is a stack of horizontal layers, held by vertical ribs. Every rib is a flat plate standing in a plane through the central axis. Layers and ribs interlock with a cross-lap: a slot is cut into the layer from its rim, a matching notch is cut into the rib, and the two slide together.

This is why a rib is always flat, always vertical, and always passes through the axis. It is also why some things are possible and others are not — most of the rules below follow from it.

Assembly

  • Inside ribs: the ribs stand in a bundle around the core. Drop each layer down over them, working from one end.
  • Outside and flush ribs: build the layer stack loosely, then slide each rib in radially from the outside until its notches engage.
  • Dry-fit the whole thing before any glue. If a joint is tight, adjust the kerf and recut — do not force it, ply splits along the grain.
  • With glue-stack (0 ribs, or skew/bend/chaos in use) there are no ribs at all: the layers simply stack and glue.

4 · The panel, in order

The panel runs in the order you should think in: what it is, what it looks like, how it is built, what it is cut from.

1 - Project

  • Flat shape gives you one cuttable piece — a coaster, a plaque, a frame if you turn on Hollow. 3D build gives you the stack. The shape carries over between them.
  • Set size fixes the diameter and height. Fit to sheets does the reverse: you say how many sheets you are willing to use, and it sizes the piece to fit, keeping the proportions.

2 - Shape

  • The seven superformula sliders. m1 and m2 set how many lobes; the n values set how sharp or swollen they are; a and b stretch the two axes.
  • Randomise leaps somewhere new. Back steps home again — it remembers the last forty states, and takes a snapshot before every randomise, preset and reset.
  • Smoother rounds a spiky form into clean curves. It filters the shape itself, not just the picture, so the slots, ribs and layers all follow the smoothed curve exactly.

3 - Structure

  • Hollow rings turns solid layers into rings. Wall is how wide the ring is. If the wall is too thick for the shape the rings collapse — the app keeps the layers solid and says so rather than deleting your piece.
  • Layers is how many horizontal slices. The app will not place them closer together than the material is thick.
  • Crop top and Crop bottom lop whole layers off the ends. The ribs shorten and re-notch themselves to whatever stack remains.
  • Solid base and Solid top cap the ends of the stack — applied to whatever the stack ends up as, so crop the top and the new top layer is the one that gets capped.
  • Ribs, Grip, Core, the placement and join options, and Min width. These get a section of their own below.

4 - Effects

  • Keeps the ribs: Scale X/Y/Z, Fractal, and Twist. A twisted stack still meets a flat plane along a straight line, so the ribs survive — they simply meet each layer at a rotated angle, and the app cuts them for it.
  • Glue-stack only: Skew, Bend, Chaos. These move each layer bodily off the axis, and no flat plane can pass through them all. Turn any of them up and the ribs are dropped; the piece becomes a glue-stack.

5 - Material and sheet

  • Material — the true thickness of your board. Measure it. 3mm ply is rarely 3mm.
  • Kerf — the width of the cut your laser makes. See Joinery.
  • Sheet width and height — the stock you are cutting from. The packer works to these.

5 · Ribs

This is where most of the craft lives.

How many, and where

  • Square joins puts each rib on a lobe. A rib meets the outline along a radial line, and that line is square to the edge only at a peak or a valley — anywhere else it crosses the flank obliquely, which looks wrong and makes a weak joint. A shape has only so many lobes, so in this mode the rib count caps there; the summary will tell you if you asked for more.
  • Even spacing ignores the lobes and spreads the ribs evenly. On a round shape it makes no difference; on a six-pointed star it can put every join 60 degrees off square.

Where they join

  • Ribs inside — a hidden spine at the core. The ribs disappear inside the piece.
  • Ribs outside — an exoskeleton. The fins stand proud of the silhouette.
  • Ribs flush — the rib's outer edge is the silhouette, mated with a half/half cross-lap: the layer is slotted half the joint depth in from its rim, the rib notched half in from its edge, so they meet exactly halfway and the rib vanishes into the outline.

What shape

  • Curved follows the form.
  • Angled is the same form in the fewest straight facets — a deliberate contrast to the curves.
  • Straight squares the rib's free edge off entirely.

Grip - the one that matters

Grip is the depth of the joint. In a cross-lap the rib's neck at each layer is exactly that deep — so grip is the strength of your rib. A 5mm grip means a 5mm neck, and a 5mm neck in 3mm ply snaps if you look at it.

Grip can only go as deep as the part allows. A ring can be cut into only as far as leaves min width of wall behind it, so a thicker wall buys you a thicker rib. The summary reports what you actually got, and flags the thinnest neck in the piece separately — that is usually a small layer near a pole, which cannot give a deep slot without being cut in half.

Min width

The thinnest anything is allowed to be. It guards the ring walls, the land beside every slot, and the body of every rib. Set it to something your material can survive — 4mm is sane for 3mm ply, more for MDF or acrylic.

6 · Joinery, kerf and fit

A laser removes material. The slot you cut comes out wider than the line you drew, by roughly the width of the beam — the kerf. If you ignore it, every joint in the piece is loose.

The app cuts every slot at thickness minus kerf, so the joint closes to a friction fit. It cannot guess your kerf: it depends on your machine, your power, your speed and your material.

Cutting a kerf test comb

  • Draw a strip with a row of slots in it, each a slightly different width: 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 3.0mm, and so on.
  • Cut it from the same board you will use for the piece, at the same settings.
  • Try a scrap of the board in each slot. The one that grips firmly but goes in by hand is your number.
  • Kerf = the nominal thickness minus the slot that fit. If 3.0mm ply fits the 2.85mm slot, your kerf is 0.15mm.
  • Do this once per material and machine, and write it on the wall.
Cut a comb before every real job. It takes two minutes and saves a whole sheet.

7 · Cut files

Download cut sheets (SVG) gives you one SVG per sheet, true to size in millimetres.

ColourMeaning
Red, 0.2mmCut line
DarkEngraved part label (L1, L2... R1, R2...)
Dashed brownThe sheet boundary - do not cut

Parts labelled L are layers, numbered from the bottom. Parts labelled R are ribs. Keep them in order as they come off the bed — a stack of near-identical rings is very hard to sort afterwards.

The packer nests parts inside the holes of hollow rings, and turns parts 90 degrees when that packs tighter. If a part is too big for one sheet it gets a sheet of its own, marked oversize.

8 · Reading the summary line

The line under the preview is the app being honest with you. Take it seriously.

It saysIt means
glue-stack - skew/bend/chaosYou are using an effect that no flat rib can follow. There are no ribs; glue the layers.
N ribs dropped (too thin here)Those ribs could not be made without breaking min width. Widen the wall, lower min width, or use fewer ribs.
N ribs over the lobe countYou asked for more square joins than the shape has lobes. Switch to Even spacing, or accept fewer ribs.
ribs stop short of N layersThose layers are too small to take a notch. The ribs end before them rather than passing through them.
joins 34 degrees off squareYour ribs are crossing the flanks obliquely. Switch on Square joins.
grip 5.0mm (all the wall allows)You asked for a deeper joint than the ring wall can give. Thicken the wall.
thinnest neck 5.2mmThe weakest joint in the piece. That is where it will break.
wall too thick to hollowThe rings collapsed. The layers have been left solid.
N croppedLayers removed from the ends.

9 · Materials

MaterialNotes
3mm birch plyThe default. Forgiving, cuts fast, holds a friction fit well.
MDFCuts beautifully, very consistent thickness, but brittle in thin sections - raise min width.
AcrylicNo grain, so thin necks survive better, but it melts: the kerf is larger and varies with speed.
Card and chipboardExcellent for a test fit at full size before you commit to timber.

Whatever you use, measure the actual thickness with callipers and cut a kerf comb from the same sheet. Two boards from the same pack can differ by a quarter of a millimetre, which is the difference between a friction fit and a rattle.

10 · When it goes wrong

SymptomCause and cure
Joints are looseKerf set too low. Measure it with a comb and raise it.
Joints will not go togetherKerf set too high, or the board is thicker than you told it. Measure both.
A rib snapped at a layerThe neck was too thin. Raise Grip; if the summary says the wall will not allow it, thicken the wall.
Ribs vanishedSkew, bend or chaos is turned up - those force a glue-stack. Or the ribs slider is at zero.
Fewer ribs than I asked forSquare joins caps at the lobe count, or ribs were dropped for being too thin. The summary says which.
The rings disappearedWall too thick for the shape. Thin the wall or smooth the shape.
Too many sheetsTry Fit to sheets, reduce layers, or use a larger sheet size.
It looks nothing like the previewCheck you did not scale the SVG on the way to the laser.

11 · A note on the shapes

Many superformula shapes are re-entrant: the surface doubles back, so two different latitudes sit at the same height. A stack of flat layers cannot reproduce an overhang, so at any height the app cuts the widest thing there — the true silhouette. This is why some presets look less like a smooth pod and more like a spool. That is the mathematics being honest, not a mistake.

If you want the gentler form, reach for the Smoother.
